Roadmap

1

Cataloguing by photo

Live

Build the collection

Browse the whole collection, and add records by photographing them and reviewing the draft before it’s saved. Hand-edit details, replace covers, and remove records one at a time. Anyone with the link can look. Only the owner can change anything, and the whole collection is backed up automatically overnight.

2

Market enrichment

Live

Discogs pricing & metadata

Every record is looked up on Discogs for accurate market prices (low, median, and high, drawn from real sales history), plus community ownership counts, ratings, and pressing details. The lowest asking price can be refreshed on demand. Median and high figures come from sales history gathered in a browser session, and they carry over when prices refresh. Every card shows all three prices, and the home page shows the total value of the collection.

3

Wishlist

Live

Track records you want to buy

Keep a separate list of records you’re hunting for, separate from your collection, at /wishlist.html. Add records by hand, or let the weekly sync import your most-played Spotify albums (vinyl pressings only). The weekly sync also imports vinyl saved in your Amazon cart, refreshes Discogs median and asking prices, and never re-adds anything you’ve deleted.

4

Listen

Live

Audio previews

Tap any record in the collection, or any item on the wishlist, to hear a short preview clip of the album’s most popular track. Previews come from Deezer. For best-of and compilation albums Deezer doesn’t carry under their own title, a small hand-picked list points each one to a representative single instead. YouTube is the last resort for anything Deezer genuinely doesn’t have. Only one preview plays at a time.

5

2026 reliability & polish

Live

Faster pages and safer backups

Every font the site uses now loads from a self-hosted file with a year-long cache instead of from Google Fonts, so pages open without waiting on Google’s servers. Steely Dan’s Aja lost its cover when the Cover Art Archive link behind it died; the replacement was checked to make sure it actually loads. Two days of nightly backups had drifted apart from the fonts work at the same time. Because the two touched entirely different files, the merge kept both sides rather than quietly discarding either.

9

Wishlist priority

Live

Sort the hunt list by price

Shipped 2026-07-29, pulled forward at the owner’s request the same way Phase 4 was. The wishlist opens cheapest first. One chip flips it to most expensive first, and a separate toggle goes back to A to Z. Whichever sort is on, every row shows how far below the historical median the current ask sits. The first version scored each item on a blend of discount and waiting time, with three presets for weighting one against the other. It was replaced the next day. Two sorts that each do exactly what their label says turned out to be worth more than one that needed explaining.

11

Concert radar

Live

See who’s touring, and who’s touring near you

A page matching artists from your collection and wishlist against real upcoming tour dates near Berkeley, CA. Coming Soon sweeps them automatically through three sources at once: SeatGeek by artist, JamBase Data as a geo-scoped sweep of the metro area (added 2026-08-13, filtered down to your own artists before it merges in), and a direct scrape of seven hand-picked Bay Area venue calendars for the box-office-only shows the other two never carry. When the same artist plays the same venue on several nights, those group into one card with a date range instead of one row per night. Watch an artist to track it before a confirmed date exists. Its shows then appear in the Watching panel with a real date, price, and ticket link, and drop out of Coming Soon rather than showing up twice. A manual “+ Add a show” fallback covers whatever none of the three sources find. Ticketmaster signup is still stuck on an account issue with their support. Bandsintown, Songkick, Eventbrite, Dice.fm, and PredictHQ were all researched and are tracked but not viable right now (see PROJECT.md’s Feeds roadmap for why). A Spotify layer stays parked, and gets added only if a real coverage gap shows up, the same discipline that put YouTube last in audio preview. Two more pieces shipped 2026-08-07. A “Traveling?” panel sweeps every artist in your collection, wishlist, and watching list against a destination city and date range, so a trip elsewhere surfaces shows you’d otherwise never see. Watching rows also gained a “Going” toggle that carries across devices once you’ve actually got tickets. Pulled forward ahead of Phase 10 at the owner’s request, the same way Phases 4 and 9 were. 7

iOS app

Live

Easy-start mobile cataloguing

Home-screen icons launch Vinyl Scout and each of the owner’s four other Claude-built projects directly, with no browser chrome and no URL to type. That part is live today, through iOS’s own “Add to Home Screen.” Still ahead is a native, camera-first record-adding flow to replace the current Safari photo upload. It hasn’t been built, and stays parked until it’s scoped.

6

Editorial polish pass

Live

Sharpen the voice; leave the palette alone

An outside design review confirmed the type, color, and spacing rules are holding, so this pass left them alone. Shipped 2026-08-07: navigation spacing tightened (22px→18px), the Audit page got darker row rules and 5px tighter padding so it reads as a working table rather than a magazine spread, and wishlist prices now stand taller than the rest of each row (11px→16px, bold). A small recurring catalog-index stamp (“№001”) and an inline instructional note were also tried on the Audit page, then pulled at the owner’s request the same week. Both read as clutter once they were live. The Guide’s prose was trimmed earlier in the same pass.

8

Close the wishlist gap

Live

Edit key on wishlist writes, remembered per device

The catalog has required an edit key to add, delete, or edit since Phase 1. Anyone with the link can look; only the owner can change anything. The Wishlist never got the same protection, because re-typing a key on mobile every session wasn’t practical for a casual hunting list, so its writes were deliberately opened up in July 2026. Built 2026-08-06: the same edit key now gates wishlist writes too, but the browser remembers it in localStorage after one entry, so it costs one entry per device instead of one per visit. The catalog’s own prompt still uses sessionStorage and only remembers for the current tab. The gap closed without the friction coming back.

10

Travel Intelligence hooks

Live

Cross-reference your trips with your music

A small, explicit hand-off between Vinyl Scout and travelintelligence.org, the owner’s other project. Travel Intelligence’s watched trips get checked against the artists you like here, within about 25 miles of the destination. That means your collection, your wishlist, and everyone you’re watching. Only the destination city and the date range cross over. No fares, no points data. A real match surfaces on both sides: “playing where you’ll be” here on the Watching panel, and a note against the relevant watched trip over on Travel Intelligence. Built 2026-08-05, the day after the technical plan (see PROJECT.md for the full design, including one deliberate deviation from it for scalability). Both ends are best-effort. If either site is briefly unreachable, the rest of the page works exactly as it always did.
