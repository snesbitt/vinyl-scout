Roadmap

Vinyl Scout is built in deliberate phases. Phases 1–5 are live; Phases 6–9 are on the horizon. Phase 4 (Listen) is the one phase pulled forward out of order, at the owner’s request; see the note below.

●1 cataloguing by photo ●2 market enrichment ●3 wishlist ●4 listen ●5 2026 reliability & polish ○6 editorial polish pass ○7 iOS app ○8 close the wishlist gap ○9 wishlist priority
1

Cataloguing by photo

Live

Build the collection

Browse the whole collection, and add records by photographing them and reviewing the draft before it’s saved. Hand-edit details, replace covers, and remove records one at a time. Editing is private; viewing is open to anyone with the link; the collection is backed up automatically.

2

Market enrichment

Live

Discogs pricing & metadata

Every record is looked up on Discogs for accurate market prices (low, median, and high, drawn from real sales history), plus community ownership counts, ratings, and pressing details. The lowest asking price can be refreshed on demand. Median and high figures come from sales history gathered in a browser session, and they carry over when prices refresh. Every card shows all three prices, and the home page shows the total value of the collection.

3

Wishlist

Live

Track records you want to buy

Keep a separate list of records you’re hunting for, separate from your collection, at /wishlist.html. Add records by hand, or let the weekly sync import your most-played Spotify albums (vinyl pressings only). The weekly sync also imports vinyl saved in your Amazon cart, refreshes Discogs median and asking prices, and never re-adds anything you’ve deleted.

4

Listen

Live

Audio previews

Tap any record in the collection, or any item on the wishlist, to hear a short preview clip of the album’s most popular track. Previews come from Deezer. For best-of and compilation albums Deezer doesn’t carry under their own title, a small hand-picked list points each one to a representative single instead. YouTube is the last resort for anything Deezer genuinely doesn’t have. Only one preview plays at a time.

5

2026 reliability & polish

Live

Faster pages, a fixed cover, safer backups

Three improvements, all shipped in 2026. Faster pages: the Fraunces and IBM Plex fonts now load from our own self-hosted files with a year-long cache, not from Google Fonts, so pages open without waiting on Google’s servers. A fixed cover: the image for Steely Dan’s Aja had gone missing when its Cover Art Archive link died, and it was replaced with an image verified to load. Safer backups: two days of automatic nightly backups had drifted apart from the fonts work, and because the two sets of changes touched entirely different files, the merge kept both sides intact rather than discarding either.

6

Editorial polish pass

Future

Sharpen the voice, not the palette

An outside design review confirmed the type, color, and spacing rules are holding, so this pass leaves them alone. It tightens navigation spacing, gives the Audit page darker rules and tighter rows so it reads as a working table rather than a magazine spread, lets wishlist prices stand a little taller than the rest of each row, and settles on one small recurring mark (a catalog stamp or numbering convention) as a signature no other page borrows. The Guide’s prose was trimmed as a first step.

7

iOS app

Future

Easy-start mobile cataloguing

A native camera-first app for adding records on the go, replacing the current Safari-based photo-upload workflow with a faster mobile entry point. Not started; parked until scoped.

8

Close the wishlist gap

Future

Edit key on wishlist writes, remembered per device

The catalog’s own add/delete/edit has required an edit key since Phase 1: viewing is open to anyone with the link, editing isn’t. The Wishlist never got the same protection: its add/delete was deliberately opened up in July 2026 because re-entering it on mobile every session wasn’t practical for a casual hunting list. As links get shared more widely, that gap is worth closing. The plan: the same edit key already protecting the catalog, but remembered in the browser after it’s entered once, so it costs one entry per device instead of one per visit, closing the gap without reintroducing the friction that got it removed in the first place.

9

Wishlist priority

Future

Rank the hunt list, not just alphabetize it

Today the wishlist sorts alphabetically by artist and nothing else. There is no scoring behind it, unlike the market data already driving the collection’s own price fields. A real priority system needs three things that do not exist yet: a weight per signal (how far the asking price sits below median, scarcity from the Discogs want/have ratio, how long an item has sat on the list), a small function that combines them, and a stored score per item, the same shape Travel Intelligence already uses for flight ranking. What stays judgment either way: how much a below-median price should outweigh scarcity is a taste call, not a data problem, and setting those weights would still be the owner’s decision, not an automated one.

Phases are normally sequential: a later phase isn’t started early, and nothing gets added to an earlier phase just because it would be easy to slot in. Phase 4 (Listen) was the one deliberate exception: pulled forward and built at the owner’s explicit request.
