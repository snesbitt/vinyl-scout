How it works · v53 charter · August 2026

A pocket catalog for a personal record collection.

Vinyl Scout is a catalog of 94 records. For each one it holds a condition grade, the exact pressing, the current market price, the Discogs community rating, and how many copies are for sale right now. Pricing and pressing data come from Discogs. Tap a record and a short preview of its most popular track plays. No accounts. Nothing on the page is tracked by a third party, and no automated job here has permission to delete a record.

This page is the reference: what the catalog does today, down to the data model and the stack. The Guide has the method behind it, and the prompt to build one for your own collection.

01What it is

A catalog for one person's records. The owner photographs covers in small batches. Claude reads each cover and identifies artist and title, and a short list of Artist - Title lines gets pasted into /seed.html to register the records. Every entry is editable on /audit.html.

Each record is enriched with Discogs data: accurate pressing ID, marketplace prices (low, median, high from sales history), community ownership and wishlist counts, and ratings. The lowest price refreshes on demand, either one record at a time or in bulk. Median and high prices come from a browser-based lookup, since Discogs blocks automated requests for that data; refreshing the low price never overwrites them.

The collection page you came from is read-only. Anyone with the link can search it and filter it by genre; nobody can edit it. Gallery view shows cover art, artist, and title. List view adds each record's low, median, and high price. Above both, the header names the single most valuable record in the collection, just under what the whole thing is worth. Tap a record for its full Discogs snapshot, its community rating, and, where one exists, a Play button.

Collection

The read-only home page. Gallery or list, filter by genre, search, open any record for the full detail.

Wishlist

Records not yet owned. Prices refresh every Monday. Adding or removing one needs the edit key.

Concert Radar

Your artists, matched against real upcoming tour dates near Berkeley, CA.

02The stack

The plumbing, for anyone who wants it. Skip ahead if you don't.

Frontend

Static HTML, CSS, one JS file. No framework, no build step. Served from Netlify's CDN.

Storage

One Netlify Blobs store, records. One JSON blob per record, keyed by ID.

API

  • Records. GET (list), POST (upsert), DELETE. Gated by an edit key.
  • Wishlist. The same three verbs at /api/wishlist, in its own store, isolated from the catalog. POST and DELETE use the same edit key as the catalog (2026-08-06). The browser remembers it after one entry, so it costs one entry per device rather than one per visit.
  • Enrichment. /api/discogs/lookup (public) and /api/discogs-pricing (gated).
  • Audio. /api/audio/preview, public, read-only. Deezer first, a small override list for compilations, then YouTube last.
  • Concert Radar. /api/tour-dates, public, read-only. SeatGeek-backed; it resolves an artist to a real performer before querying events near Berkeley, CA. An optional city override (2026-08-07) lets the “Traveling?” panel sweep a different destination.
  • Concert Radar, JamBase feed. /api/jambase-shows, public, read-only, added 2026-08-13. One geo-scoped sweep of real ticketing data, not per-artist, resolved to the containing metro area. The browser then filters it down to catalog, wishlist, and watching artists before anything merges into Coming Soon.
  • Concert Radar, reverse lookup. /api/artists-playing, public, read-only, CORS-enabled for travelintelligence.org. Give it a destination and a date window and it returns real nearby events cross-referenced against this catalog, wishlist, and watching list. This is the query Travel Intelligence's watched trips run.
  • Watching. /api/watching, POST, ungated — deliberately, and reviewed again on 2026-08-21 when the wishlist’s own gate went in. What it stores is artist, optional city, and a going flag: a notification preference, not a shopping list, in its own isolated store. The catalog, covers, and wishlist all stay gated. The going flag (2026-08-07) carries a confirmed-ticket toggle across devices.
  • Concert Radar, weekly cache. /api/catalog-cache, public, read-only. Serves the merged show list the Sunday sweep commits to data/catalog-cache.json, so Concert Radar’s first paint doesn’t wait on three live feeds. Since 2026-08-19 it reads that committed file rather than Blobs, which means its data refreshes on deploy. Carries artistCount and an at timestamp; a freshness check watches that timestamp.
  • Concert Radar, venue scrape. /api/venue-shows, public, read-only. Seven hand-picked Bay Area venues, scraped by venue rather than by artist, for the box-office-only shows SeatGeek and JamBase don’t carry. Returns every show at those venues; the client filters down to catalog, wishlist, and watching artists before merging.
  • Scheduled work. The Sunday sweep runs in GitHub Actions, not as a Function — it reads the same public endpoints and commits its merged result, holding no credential of its own. Nightly catalog and watching backups run the same way. A Monday health check reads the live site; when it finds a Concert Radar problem it opens a pull request rather than writing to main.

Source

Plain Git repo at github.com/snesbitt/vinyl-scout. git push deploys; Netlify rebuilds in under a minute.

Authoring

Code and copy written with Claude. Every deploy is reviewed by the owner and pushed from her own machine, whether she runs the push herself or tells a session to run one she has approved.

03The catalog record

Every record is one JSON object. Phase 1 fields are the originals; Phase 2 (live) adds Discogs enrichment: condition, accurate pressing ID, marketplace pricing, community data, and ratings.

  • id: opaque 16-character hex string (e.g. rec_a1b2c3d4e5f60718).
  • artist, title: required strings.
  • year, genre: optional. Year is 1900–2100.
  • cover_url: optional image URL or inline data URL.
  • notes: optional free text.
  • condition: Goldmine grade (default VG). See below.
  • price_low, price_median, price_high: market prices in price_currency, optional. Low is the current cheapest listing; median and high are from the release's last-30-sales history.
  • price_last_sold: string date of the most recent completed sale (e.g. "Apr 23, 2026"), or null if the release has never sold.
  • copies_available: count of copies currently for sale, optional.
  • have_count, want_count: Discogs community ownership and wishlist counts, optional.
  • rating_avg, rating_count: Discogs community rating (0–5) and number of ratings, optional.
  • price_currency: ISO code (USD, EUR, GBP), optional.
  • price_updated_at: ISO timestamp of last refresh, optional.
  • discogs_release_id: cached release ID for fast refreshes, optional.

04Condition grades

Vinyl Scout uses Goldmine grading (the US standard for record sleeves and discs). Each record stores its own condition code. Default is VG (Very Good).

  • M: Mint. Never played, still sealed or in perfect condition.
  • NM: Near Mint. Played once or twice, looks and sounds unplayed.
  • VG+: Very Good Plus. Minor wear; fully playable with no defects.
  • VG: Very Good. Light surface marks, plays without issue.
  • G+: Good Plus. Noticeable wear, plays through, some surface noise.
  • G: Good. Significant wear, plays but with some noise.
  • F: Fair. Heavy wear, plays but may skip or have extended noise.
  • P: Poor. Severely damaged, barely playable.

05Wishlist

A separate hunt list at /wishlist.html, kept apart from the collection. Add a record by hand; its cover art and Discogs reference get looked up for you. The rest arrive on their own: a weekly sync reads the owner’s most-played Spotify albums, keeps only the ones that exist on vinyl, and skips anything already owned. That same sync reads the owner’s Amazon cart, and never changes it.

Each row shows the album art, the current asking price or Discogs’ median sale price, and a link to current listings. A brand-new item shows no price at all until the following Monday's scout run.

Adding and removing items needs the same edit key as the collection. The browser remembers it after one entry, so it costs one entry per device rather than one per visit (2026-08-06). Before that the list was open to anyone with the link, because re-typing a key on mobile every session wasn't worth it for a list used this casually. Remembering the key closed the gap without bringing the friction back.

The weekly scout refreshes prices. Nothing is auto-removed, and anything deleted by hand is never auto-re-added.

Sorting is deliberately literal. The default puts the cheapest item first, a chip flips that to most expensive first, and a separate control sorts A to Z instead. Every row carries a discount badge whenever the current ask sits below the historical median, in any sort. An earlier version scored each item on a blend of discount and waiting time; it lasted a day. Two sorts that each do exactly what their label says turned out to be worth more. See Phase 9.

06Audio preview

Every record's detail view has a Play button. It finds the album's most popular track and plays a short clip in place. Deezer is the first place it looks, and no sign-up is needed. Best-of and compilation albums that Deezer doesn't carry under their own title fall back to a small hand-picked list pointing at a representative single by the same artist. YouTube is the last resort. A credit line under the player names whichever source answered.

Nothing is stored. If no source has a match or a playable clip, the page says so quietly.

07Concert Radar

A separate page, /concert-radar.html, matches every artist in the collection and wishlist against real upcoming tour dates near Berkeley, CA. Three sources feed it: SeatGeek, JamBase Data, and a direct scrape of seven hand-picked Bay Area venue calendars. Coming Soon refreshes on its own. When the same artist plays the same venue on several nights, those group into one card with a date range instead of one row per night.

Watch an artist to track it before a confirmed date exists. A watched artist's real date, price, and ticket link then show up in the Watching panel, and its shows drop out of Coming Soon so nothing appears twice. Price isn't always available. SeatGeek doesn't publish it for every show, most often ones announced far enough ahead that resale listings haven't opened yet. Ticketmaster and a Spotify layer stay parked, and get added only if a real coverage gap shows up.

Two more pieces shipped 2026-08-07. A “Traveling?” panel takes a city and a date range and sweeps every distinct artist across the collection, wishlist, and watching list against that destination instead of Berkeley, so a trip elsewhere surfaces shows you'd otherwise never see. Watching rows also gained a “Going” toggle. Once you've actually got tickets, marking it carries across devices and puts a badge next to the artist name.

Since 2026-08-05 it also runs in reverse. Travel Intelligence (the owner's other project) checks its own watched trips against this same catalog, wishlist, and watching list, within about 25 miles of the destination. It sends the destination and the date range, nothing else. No fares, no points data. A real match shows up on both sides: here in the Watching panel with a link back to the trip, and against the relevant trip over on travelintelligence.org. Both ends are best-effort. If either site is briefly unreachable, the rest of the page works exactly as it always did.

08Data & backups

Every record is backed up automatically. A nightly job commits the whole catalog to backups/YYYY-MM-DD.json in the GitHub repo. A backup can also run on demand.

The catalog is read-only for the public; anyone can browse it. Edits require an edit key. There are no user accounts.

09What it won't do

  • Reading the catalog requires nothing at all. Edits need an edit key, never a login, and there are no user accounts or passwords anywhere in it.
  • No analytics, advertising, or third-party tracking.
  • Nothing on the wishlist is auto-removed, and anything deleted by hand is never auto-re-added.
  • The data is never sold or shared.
